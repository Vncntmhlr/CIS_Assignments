\section*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Abstract}
A tracker-based calibration workflow is developed for a Cartesian robot with a spherical wrist. A tetrahedral marker body and nominal visibility envelope guide scene design. Five homings and 765 observations across 69 single-axis configurations initialize a 34-parameter geometric and quadratic joint-distortion model. A fixed gauge leaves 33 optimized parameters; full-interval magnitude and derivative constraints enforce known joint-error bounds. Independent calibrations of seeds 1, 7, and 61 each require approximately 554 s simulated acquisition and 64 s wall-clock fitting. The preserved Seed 1 protocols pass 729/729 FK configurations and 40/40 physical IK targets. Supplementary testing passes 3960/3960 physical FK configurations and 240/240 random executed IK targets, with respective overall maxima of 0.12485 mm / $4.98\times10^{-4}$ rad and 0.13286 mm / $7.98\times10^{-4}$ rad. Stress tests expose substantial limitations of the unchanged provided inverse: only 90/180 targets pass both accuracy criteria. Tracker noise, gauge-dependent parameter recovery, and finite workspace coverage remain explicit limitations.

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:introduction}
\subsection{Background and Problem Statement}
Accurate robot positioning requires a kinematic model that accounts for departures from nominal geometry and encoder readings. This assignment calibrates a Cartesian robot with three prismatic joints and a three-axis spherical wrist, using an optical tracker and an end-effector calibration body. Base-placement error, axis misalignment, tool-frame error, and systematic joint distortion cause the physical robot to depart from its nominal model. External tracker observations provide the measurements needed to estimate these corrections.

\subsection{Objectives and Performance Requirements}
The assignment asks for improved forward and inverse kinematics over the work volume, with the performance requirements summarized in \cref{tab:requirements}.
\begin{table}[!htbp]\centering
\caption{Accuracy and execution requirements from the Assignment 2 handout.}\label{tab:requirements}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lll@{}}\toprule
Criterion & Required bound & Evaluation note\\\midrule
Position accuracy (FK and IK) & \SI{0.2}{\milli\metre} & Workspace-level evaluation\\
Orientation accuracy (FK and IK) & \SI{1e-3}{\radian} & Workspace-level evaluation\\
Program execution & \SI{10}{\minute} & Instructor runtime limit\\\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}
RMS and maximum position/orientation errors are reported separately, together with the fraction of attempted targets meeting both accuracy bounds. An acceptable average does not establish accuracy at every pose. Simulated acquisition/testing time and wall-clock computation time are recorded separately; the ten-minute program-execution limit must not be confused with the simulator's elapsed-time counter.

\subsection{Summary of the Proposed Approach}
We follow the recommended parameter-estimation route (Method~1): repeated homing establishes joint offsets, individual joint sweeps supply calibration observations, and physically constrained nonlinear least squares fits a corrected geometric model with smooth joint-distortion terms. This avoids a dense six-dimensional calibration grid. Evaluation distinguishes tracker prediction, physical FK accuracy, numerical FK/IK consistency, and executed physical IK accuracy. Seeds 1, 7, and 61 are calibrated independently, with ordinary-workspace and stress-test results kept separate. Hidden simulator values are used only for evaluation; the provided inverse solver is retained so its numerical limitations can be distinguished from forward-model accuracy.

\section{System Design and Calibration Setup}\label{sec:design}
\subsection{Robot and Optical Tracker}
The first three joints translate the wrist center along the nominal base axes; the final three rotate a spherical wrist. The joint ranges and speed limits are listed in \cref{tab:joint_limits}. Motion time includes acceleration-limited travel and \SI{0.05}{\second} settling, making wrist travel particularly expensive.

\begin{tabular}{@{}lll@{}}\toprule
Joints & Permitted range & Maximum speed\\\midrule
$1,3$ & $[0,600]$ mm & \SI{50}{\milli\metre\per\second}\\
$2$ & $[-300,300]$ mm & \SI{50}{\milli\metre\per\second}\\
$4,5$ & $[-3\pi/4,3\pi/4]$ rad & \SI{0.1}{\radian\per\second}\\
$6$ & $[-\pi,\pi]$ rad & \SI{0.1}{\radian\per\second}\\\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

The tracker has a square viewing frustum extending from \SI{1000}{\milli\metre} to \SI{2200}{\milli\metre} along its local $+z$ axis. Marker measurements include independent per-coordinate noise with standard deviation \SI{0.1}{\milli\metre} and spatially varying systematic error. Camera-pose jiggle is disabled by the assignment, which does not make the marker observations noiseless. Each snapshot costs \SI{0.03}{\second} of simulated time.

\subsection{Coordinate Frames and Nominal Geometry}
The nominal robot model is expressed as:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:nominal_fk}
F_{BE}^{\mathrm{nom}}(\thv)
=F_c(\theta_1,\theta_2,\theta_3)\,F_{cw}\,F_w(\theta_4,\theta_5,\theta_6)\,F_{we}.
\end{equation}
Let $W$, $T$, $B$, and $E$ denote workspace, tracker, robot-base, and end-effector frames. The convention $F_{AB}={}^AT_B$ maps coordinates from $B$ into $A$. In \cref{eq:nominal_fk}, $F_c=\operatorname{Trans}(\theta_1,\theta_2,\theta_3)$, $F_{cw}=\operatorname{Trans}(500,0,0)$, and $F_{we}=\operatorname{Trans}(100,0,0)$, with translations in millimeters; $F_w$ applies the ordered rotations $R_x(\theta_4)R_y(\theta_5)R_z(\theta_6)$. Rotations are embedded in homogeneous transformations. The tracker-frame prediction is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tracker_chain}
\widehat F_{TE}(\thv)=\widehat F_{TB}\,F_{BE}(\thv),
\qquad F_{TB}=F_{WT}^{-1}F_{WB}.
\end{equation}
The estimated base-to-tracker transform is applied once on the left; the nominal or corrected $F_{BE}$ describes the robot's internal kinematics.

\subsection{Calibration Marker Body Design}
Four markers form a regular tetrahedron centered at the end-effector origin, with coordinates given in \cref{tab:markers}. Each marker has radius $37.5\sqrt{3}=\SI{64.95}{\milli\metre}$, below the \SI{75}{\milli\metre} limit. This symmetric, noncoplanar layout spreads the observations in three dimensions and avoids favoring one rotational direction. The special calibration body's geometry is exact under the TA API, and its frame coincides with $E$. Therefore, no marker-manufacturing refinement or separate marker-to-tool transform is estimated.

\subsection{Camera Placement and Visibility Analysis}
The tracker frame coincides with the workspace frame and looks along $+z$. The chosen approximate robot-base pose is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:scene_placement}
F_{WT}=I,\qquad F_{WB}^{\mathrm{approx}}=\operatorname{Trans}(-800,0,1300),
\end{equation}
with translation coordinates in millimeters. Including the fixed base-to-wrist offset, nominal wrist-center travel is $x,y\in[-300,300]$ mm and $z\in[1300,1900]$ mm, centered at $(0,0,1600)$ mm.

For any wrist orientation, the tool length and marker radius bound the distance from wrist center to a marker by $\rho=100+64.95=\SI{164.95}{\milli\metre}$. Thus a conservative marker envelope is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:marker_envelope}
|x|,|y|\leq\SI{464.95}{\milli\metre},\qquad
z\in[1135.05,2064.95]\ \mathrm{mm}.
\end{equation}
The frustum half-width is $h(z)=500+(500/1200)(z-1000)$, with $z$ and $h$ in millimeters. At the envelope's nearest depth, $h=\SI{556.27}{\milli\metre}$, giving at least \SI{91.32}{\milli\metre} lateral clearance and approximately \SI{135.05}{\milli\metre} near/far clearance. This bound covers the complete nominal joint range, including rotations between sampled poses. It is a nominal geometric result, not a guarantee under every unknown physical perturbation.

\subsection{Design Verification and Unit Tests}
Design checks require all markers to satisfy the radius constraint, the centered layout to span three dimensions, every planned command to satisfy joint limits, and every nominal marker to remain in the tracker frustum. All 81 initial visualization poses pass visibility checks. An additional sweep using three values for each linear joint and five for each wrist joint retains all markers at $3^3\times5^3=3375/3375$ nominal poses. These numerical checks support the analytic envelope; they do not establish calibration accuracy.

The acquisition setup uses five homings, ten mixed baseline observations, and 765 calibration observations across 69 distinct single-axis configurations. Eleven re-commanded readings per configuration, plus return-reference readings, renew both tracker and robot repeatability noise. Each valid tracker frame is paired with its settled encoder reading; missing observations are not replaced by stale data. The same design is applied independently to each seed, costing approximately \SIrange{554.03}{554.06}{\second} simulated time including homing and baseline measurements. Sampling-density, repetition, and estimator derivations are developed in \cref{sec:math}.

\section{Mathematical Methods}\label{sec:math
}
\subsection{Frames, Coordinates, and Units}

A transformation ${}^{A}F_B=[R,p]$ maps coordinates
from frame $B$ into frame $A$:
\[
{}^{A}p_P=R\,{}^{B}p_P+p.
\]
Transform composition satisfies
\[
[R_a,p_a][R_b,p_b]
=
[R_aR_b,\;p_a+R_ap_b].
\]

The frames are workspace $\{0\}$, tracker
$\{\mathrm{trk}\}$, robot base $\{\mathrm{rob}\}$,
carriage $\{c\}$, wrist input $\{w_0\}$,
wrist output $\{w\}$, and tool $\{ee\}$.

Define
\[
F_X={}^{\mathrm{trk}}F_{\mathrm{rob}}.
\]

The forward-kinematic chain is
\[
\begin{aligned}
F_{ee}(\theta_1,\ldots,\theta_6)
&=
F_c(\theta_1,\theta_2,\theta_3)F_{cw}\\
&\quad{} F_w(\theta_4,\theta_5,\theta_6)F_{we}.
\end{aligned}
\]

Joints $1$--$3$ are linear coordinates in millimetres;
joints $4$--$6$ are angles in radians.

The component transformations are
\[
F_c=
\begin{bmatrix}
I&\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{3}\theta_i d_i\\
0&1
\end{bmatrix},
\qquad
F_w=
\begin{bmatrix}
R_w&0\\
0&1
\end{bmatrix},
\]
where
\[
R_w=
R(n_4,\theta_4)
R(n_5,\theta_5)
R(n_6,\theta_6).
\]

Here, $d_i$ and $n_i$ are unit axis directions,
and $R(a,\theta)$ denotes rotation through $\theta$
about unit axis $a$.

For the nominal identity rotation in $F_{cw}$,
\[
R_{ee}=R_wR_{we},
\qquad
p_{ee}=
\sum_{i=1}^{3}\theta_i d_i+p_{cw}+R_wp_{we}.
\]

The nominal fixed translations are
\[
p_{cw}=(500,0,0)^T\,\mathrm{mm},
\qquad
p_{we}=(100,0,0)^T\,\mathrm{mm}.
\]

Tracker observations and predictions must be compared
in the same frame:
\[
F_{ee,j,\mathrm{pred}}^{\mathrm{trk}}
=
F_XF_{ee}^{\mathrm{rob}}
(\boldsymbol{\theta}_{\mathrm{cmd},j}).
\]

Scene placements provide the initial relationship
\[
F_{X,\mathrm{approx}}
=
\left({}^{0}F_{\mathrm{trk}}\right)^{-1}
{}^{0}F_{\mathrm{rob}}.
\]

A candidate initialization from observation $j$ is
\[
F_{X,j}
\approx
F_{ee,j,\mathrm{meas}}^{\mathrm{trk}}
\left[
F_{ee,\mathrm{est}}^{\mathrm{rob}}
(\boldsymbol{\theta}_{\mathrm{cmd},j})
\right]^{-1}.
\]
Rotation-aware averaging is required when
consolidating rigid transformations.

\subsection{Joint-Coordinate Correction Convention}

For joint $i$, define:
\begin{itemize}
\item $\theta_{i,\mathrm{cmd}}$:
commanded or encoder coordinate;
\item $\theta_{i,\mathrm{est}}$:
uncalibrated model estimate, initially equal
to the command;
\item $\theta_{i,\mathrm{meas}}$:
joint coordinate inferred from tracker-observed
tool motion, when available;
\item $\Delta\theta_i$:
measured-minus-estimated correction.
\end{itemize}

Thus,
\[
\Delta\theta_i
=
\theta_{i,\mathrm{meas}}
-
\theta_{i,\mathrm{est}},
\]
and
\[
\theta_{i,\mathrm{cal}}
=
\theta_{i,\mathrm{est}}
+
\widehat{\Delta\theta}_i
(\theta_{i,\mathrm{cmd}}).
\]

The tracker observes tool poses, not joint angles
directly. Isolated sweeps can support joint-coordinate
inference. Alternatively, the joint model can be
fitted directly against tool-position residuals without
recovering measured joint coordinates for every
observation.

Updating the prediction does not alter the original
robot command.


\subsection{Model Calibration}

The objective of model calibration is to improve the accuracy
of the nominal forward-kinematic model by accounting for
systematic errors in joint coordinates, joint-axis directions,
robot-base placement, and tool mounting.

The nominal forward-kinematic model is defined as:

\begin{equation}
F_{BE}^{nom}(\boldsymbol{\theta})
=
F_c(\theta_1,\theta_2,\theta_3)
F_{cw}
F_w(\theta_4,\theta_5,\theta_6)
F_{we}
\end{equation}

where $F_c$ represents the Cartesian carriage translation,
$F_{cw}$ describes the fixed carriage-to-wrist transformation,
$F_w$ represents the three-axis spherical wrist rotation,
and $F_{we}$ describes the fixed wrist-to-end-effector
transformation.

The calibrated model introduces joint-coordinate corrections,
axis misalignment corrections, and fixed rigid-body
transformations. Their parameters are estimated using
tracker-observed end-effector poses.

\subsubsection{Joint-Coordinate Correction}

For each joint $i$, the commanded coordinate
$\theta_{i,cmd}$ may differ from the actual coordinate
due to home offsets and systematic joint distortion.

The calibrated joint coordinate is defined as:

\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\theta_{i,cal}
=
\theta_{i,cmd}
+
\widehat{\Delta\theta}_i
}
\end{equation}

The modeled correction consists of a measured home offset
$o_i$ and a polynomial distortion function:

\begin{equation}
\widehat{\Delta\theta}_i
=
o_i+
\sum_{m=1}^{n_{poly}}c_{i,m}u_i^m
\end{equation}

where the normalized coordinate is:

\begin{equation}
\boxed{
u_i=
\frac{\theta_{i,cmd}+o_i}{s_i}
}
\end{equation}

with the fixed normalization scale:

\begin{equation}
s_i=
\max\left(
|\theta_{i,\min}|,
|\theta_{i,\max}|
\right)
\end{equation}

For the quadratic model, $n_{poly}=2$, giving:

\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\theta_{i,cal}
=
\theta_{i,cmd}
+
o_i
+
c_{i,1}u_i
+
c_{i,2}u_i^2
}
\end{equation}

The home offsets are determined separately through
the homing procedure, while the polynomial coefficients
are estimated during model fitting.

The normalization anchors the variable distortion terms
at the measured home position. For
$\theta_{i,cmd}=-o_i$, the corrected coordinate satisfies:

\begin{equation}
\theta_{i,cal}(-o_i)=0
\end{equation}

The correction model is applied to all six joints.
Joints 1--3 represent prismatic motion in millimeters,
while joints 4--6 represent rotational motion in radians.


\subsubsection{Joint-Axis Misalignment Correction}

In addition to joint-coordinate distortion, the physical
joint axes may deviate from their nominal orientations.

Let $\mathbf{a}_i$ denote a nominal unit axis direction,
corresponding to $\mathbf{d}_i$ for a prismatic joint
or $\mathbf{n}_i$ for a revolute joint.

Two orthonormal vectors $\mathbf{u}_i$ and
$\mathbf{v}_i$ are selected perpendicular to
the nominal axis such that:

\begin{equation}
\mathbf{u}_i^T\mathbf{a}_i=0,
\qquad
\mathbf{v}_i^T\mathbf{a}_i=0,
\qquad
\mathbf{u}_i^T\mathbf{v}_i=0
\end{equation}

The axis-tilt vector is parameterized by two
independent angular coefficients:

\begin{equation}
\boldsymbol{\phi}_i
=
\alpha_i\mathbf{u}_i
+
\beta_i\mathbf{v}_i
\end{equation}

The corrected joint-axis direction is calculated using
the rotation exponential:

\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\mathbf{a}_i'
=
\exp\left(
[\boldsymbol{\phi}_i]_{\times}
\right)\mathbf{a}_i
}
\end{equation}

where $[\boldsymbol{\phi}_i]_{\times}$ denotes the
skew-symmetric matrix of the axis-tilt vector.

Only two parameters are required because rotation
about the nominal axis does not change its direction.

The first prismatic joint axis is assumed exact.
Consequently, the calibration estimates two tilt
parameters for each of the remaining five axes,
resulting in ten joint-axis parameters.


\subsubsection{Base-Frame and Tool-Mounting Corrections}

The calibration model also accounts for uncertainties
in the transformation between the robot base and
optical tracker, together with errors in the fixed
wrist-to-end-effector transformation.

Let $F_{X,0}$ denote the initial base-to-tracker
transformation estimate.

The calibrated base transformation is defined as:

\begin{equation}
\boxed{
F_X=
F_{X,0}
\exp\left(
\widehat{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_X
\right)
}
\end{equation}

where $\boldsymbol{\xi}_X\in\mathbb{R}^6$ contains
three rotational and three translational
twist parameters.

Similarly, the calibrated wrist-to-end-effector
transformation is:

\begin{equation}
\boxed{
F_{we,cal}
=
F_{we}
\exp\left(
\widehat{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{we}
\right)
}
\end{equation}

where $\boldsymbol{\xi}_{we}\in\mathbb{R}^6$
represents the fixed tool-mounting correction.

Both corrections are represented using the
$SE(3)$ exponential map to preserve the
rigid-body transformation structure.


\subsubsection{Corrected Forward-Kinematic Model}

The corrected Cartesian carriage transformation is:

\begin{equation}
F_{c,cal}
=
\operatorname{Trans}
\left(
\sum_{i=1}^{3}
\theta_{i,cal}\mathbf{d}_i'
\right)
\end{equation}

where $\mathbf{d}_1'=\mathbf{d}_1$, while
$\mathbf{d}_2'$ and $\mathbf{d}_3'$ are
the corrected prismatic-axis directions.

The corrected wrist transformation is:

\begin{equation}
F_{w,cal}
=
\begin{bmatrix}
R_{w,cal} & \mathbf{0}\\
\mathbf{0}^T & 1
\end{bmatrix}
\end{equation}

where:

\begin{equation}
R_{w,cal}
=
\prod_{i=4}^{6}
R(\mathbf{n}_i',\theta_{i,cal})
\end{equation}

Combining the corrected components gives:

\begin{equation}
\boxed{
F_{BE}^{cal}(\boldsymbol{\theta})
=
F_{c,cal}
F_{cw}
F_{w,cal}
F_{we,cal}
}
\end{equation}

The predicted end-effector pose in the tracker
coordinate frame is:

\begin{equation}
\boxed{
F_{TE}^{pred}
=
F_XF_{BE}^{cal}(\boldsymbol{\theta})
}
\end{equation}


\subsubsection{Model Parameterization and Optimization}

The calibration parameters are assembled into
a single optimization vector:

\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\mathbf{p}
=
\begin{bmatrix}
\boldsymbol{\xi}_X\\
\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{axis}\\
\boldsymbol{\xi}_{we}\\
\mathbf{c}
\end{bmatrix}
}
\end{equation}

where $\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{axis}\in\mathbb{R}^{10}$
contains the joint-axis tilt parameters, and
$\mathbf{c}\in\mathbb{R}^{6n_{poly}}$ contains
the joint-distortion polynomial coefficients.

The total number of optimization parameters is:

\begin{equation}
\boxed{
n_p=6+10+6+6n_{poly}
=22+6n_{poly}
}
\end{equation}

For the quadratic model:

\begin{equation}
\boxed{
n_p=34
}
\end{equation}

The correction/optimization vectors as summarized in code can be found here in appendix \cref{tab:parameter_layout}. 

For each observation $j$, the calibrated kinematic
model predicts an end-effector pose
$F_{ee,j}^{pred}(\mathbf{p})$, which is compared
with the tracker-observed pose $F_{ee,j}^{meas}$.

The rotational and translational discrepancies
are defined as:

\begin{equation}
\Delta R_j
=
R_{ee,j}^{meas}
(R_{ee,j}^{pred})^T
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\Delta\mathbf{t}_j
=
\mathbf{t}_{ee,j}^{meas}
-
\mathbf{t}_{ee,j}^{pred}
\end{equation}

The weighted six-dimensional pose residual is:

\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\mathbf{r}_j(\mathbf{p})
=
\begin{bmatrix}
\operatorname{Log}_{SO(3)}(\Delta R_j)/\sigma_R\\
\Delta\mathbf{t}_j/\sigma_t
\end{bmatrix}
}
\end{equation}

where $\sigma_R=0.001$ rad and
$\sigma_t=0.2$ mm are the orientation and
position normalization scales, respectively.

The residuals from all $N$ observations are stacked:

\begin{equation}
\mathbf{r}(\mathbf{p})
=
\begin{bmatrix}
\mathbf{r}_1(\mathbf{p})\\
\mathbf{r}_2(\mathbf{p})\\
\vdots\\
\mathbf{r}_N(\mathbf{p})
\end{bmatrix}
\in\mathbb{R}^{6N}
\end{equation}

The model parameters are estimated by solving
the nonlinear least-squares problem. We use the same prescudes mathematical approach for solving linear least :

\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\hat{\mathbf{p}}
=
\underset{\mathbf{p}}{\operatorname{argmin}}
\sum_{j=1}^{N}
\left\|\mathbf{r}_j(\mathbf{p})\right\|_2^2
}
\end{equation}

The estimated parameter vector is subsequently used
to construct the corrected forward-kinematic model.


\subsection{Pose Residual Convention and Fitting Objective}

Use prediction-inverse times observation, with both
poses expressed in tracker coordinates:
\[
\begin{aligned}
\Delta F_j
&=
\left(
F_{ee,j,\mathrm{pred}}^{\mathrm{trk}}
\right)^{-1}
F_{ee,j,\mathrm{meas}}^{\mathrm{trk}}\\
&=
\begin{bmatrix}
R_{\mathrm{err},j}&p_{\mathrm{err},j}\\
0&1
\end{bmatrix}.
\end{aligned}
\]

This convention satisfies
\[
F_{ee,j,\mathrm{meas}}^{\mathrm{trk}}
=
F_{ee,j,\mathrm{pred}}^{\mathrm{trk}}
\Delta F_j.
\]

Let $\alpha_j$ be the three-component rotation vector
obtained from $R_{\mathrm{err},j}$, and let
\[
\epsilon_j=p_{\mathrm{err},j}.
\]

The translation residual is expressed in the
predicted tool frame. This representation combines
a rotation logarithm and a translation column;
it is not the full $SE(3)$ logarithm.

Define
\[
r_j=
\begin{bmatrix}
\alpha_j/(10^{-3}\,\mathrm{rad})\\
\epsilon_j/(0.2\,\mathrm{mm})
\end{bmatrix},
\qquad
r(\boldsymbol{p})=
\begin{bmatrix}
r_1\\
\vdots\\
r_N
\end{bmatrix}.
\]

The fitted correction vector is
\[
\boxed{
\hat{\boldsymbol{p}}
=
\underset{\boldsymbol{p}}{\mathrm{argmin}}
\left\|r(\boldsymbol{p})\right\|^2
}
\]

Here, $\boldsymbol{p}$ denotes the flat
correction-parameter vector, distinct from a
transform translation $p$, and $N$ counts
observed poses.

The denominators balance radians and millimetres
using assignment targets, not assumed noise
standard deviations.

Observation-inverse times prediction gives the
inverse error transformation. Reversing a rigid
transformation generally rotates as well as
negates its translation. The prediction-inverse
convention above supersedes the earlier
observation-inverse convention in the working notes.

\subsection{Linear Interpolation and Sample Spacing}

For adjacent commanded coordinates $a$ and $b$, let
\[
h_i=b-a,
\qquad
\lambda=
\frac{\theta_{i,\mathrm{cmd}}-a}{h_i},
\qquad
0\leq\lambda\leq1.
\]

The interpolated correction is
\[
\widehat{\Delta\theta}_i
=
(1-\lambda)\Delta\theta_i(a)
+
\lambda\Delta\theta_i(b).
\]

The horizontal graph axis is the commanded
coordinate; the vertical axis is the
measured-minus-estimated correction.
Both use millimetres for a linear joint
and radians for a wrist joint.

Assume exact endpoint corrections and
\[
\left|
\frac{d\Delta\theta_i}{d\theta_{i,\mathrm{cmd}}}
\right|
\leq\omega_{\mathrm{sys},i}.
\]

Then
\[
\begin{aligned}
\left|
\Delta\theta_i-\widehat{\Delta\theta}_i
\right|
&\leq
2\omega_{\mathrm{sys},i}h_i
\lambda(1-\lambda)\\
&\leq
\frac{\omega_{\mathrm{sys},i}h_i}{2}.
\end{aligned}
\]

An allocated per-joint interpolation allowance
$\varepsilon_i$ gives
\[
h_{i,\max}
=
\frac{2\varepsilon_i}{\omega_{\mathrm{sys},i}},
\]
and
\[
N_i=
\left\lceil
\frac{\theta_{i,\max}-\theta_{i,\min}}
     {h_{i,\max}}
\right\rceil+1.
\]

A pointwise bound also bounds RMS error.
However, an RMS target alone does not provide
the derivative bound needed to determine spacing.
Axis-tilt magnitude and correction-slope bounds
are distinct quantities.

\subsection{Proposed Accuracy Budget}

For wrist-joint spacing, the proposed allowance is
\[
\varepsilon_i
=
\frac{0.5\,\texttt{desired\_accuracy}}{3},
\qquad i=4,5,6.
\]

This allocates half the orientation-error target
conservatively among three wrist correction
functions. It is a tunable planning allocation,
not an assignment requirement or a guarantee
of total pose accuracy. Other error sources
must fit within the remaining budget.

Linear joints require a separate position-error
allowance in millimetres. An analogous proposed
allocation assigns half the position target
among the three linear joints.

Wrist errors also affect tool position through
the tool offset. Consequently, these separate
allowances alone do not establish a complete
tool-position error guarantee.

\subsection{Physically Constrained Nonlinear Fitting}
For each observed/predicted pair, retain signed components:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:least_squares}
r_j(\betav)=\begin{bmatrix}
\operatorname{Log}_{SO(3)}(\Delta R_j)/0.001\\
\Delta p_j/0.2
\end{bmatrix},\qquad
\widehat\betav=\arg\min_{\betav\in\mathcal C}\frac12\sum_{j=1}^{765}\|r_j(\betav)\|^2.
\end{equation}
These target-based weights are dimensionless, not covariance whitening. Scalar error magnitudes alone would discard directional information. All 765 observations enter the objective individually; no averaging or truth-based tuning is used in fitting.

Joint-error specifications impose, throughout each permitted encoder interval,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:physical_constraints}
 |\delta_k(\theta)|\leq D_k,\qquad
 |\delta'_k(\theta)|\leq\Omega_k,
\end{equation}
For joints 1--3, $D_k=0.2$ mm and $\Omega_k=5\times10^{-4}$; for joints 4--6, $D_k=0.01$ rad and $\Omega_k=5\times10^{-5}$. Quadratic magnitude extrema occur at interval endpoints or an interior stationary point; the derivative is linear, so its extrema occur at the endpoints. Constraints are therefore checked over the full interval analytically, including the home anchor.

Following the classroom parameter-estimation framework, optimization uses dimensionless parameter scales: 0.01 rad for angular corrections, 1 mm for base translation, 0.5 mm for tool translation, and 0.2 mm / 0.01 rad for linear/angular distortion coefficients. An initial residual-Jacobian SVD further preconditions the 33 free coordinates. Scaled SLSQP uses $\mathrm{ftol}=10^{-10}$ and a 100-evaluation/iteration budget, with central numerical residual derivatives and analytic constraint gradients. Dividing the objective by sample count changes scaling, not its minimizer. The parameter vector is updated only after successful, finite, physically feasible optimization. Historical unconstrained trust-region fits remain separate comparisons.

\subsection{Identifiability and Gauge Freedom}
Identifiability asks whether different calibration parameter values produce distinguishable observations. The residual Jacobian is a sensitivity table: it records how small parameter changes affect the predicted measurement errors. Using dimensionless coordinates $z_i=\beta_i/S_i$, where $S_i$ are the parameter scales defined above,
\begin{equation*}
\Delta r \simeq J_z\Delta z,
\qquad
J_z=\frac{\partial r}{\partial z}=U\Sigma V^T.
\end{equation*}
The singular value decomposition (SVD) identifies parameter combinations and their sensitivity magnitudes, the singular values. A large singular value indicates an easily observed change; a small value indicates weak sensitivity, and a zero value indicates an unobservable parameter combination. Numerical rank counts singular values above a chosen threshold.

Central differences use scaled steps $h_i=10^{-4}\max(1,|z_i|)$ and $2h_i$ to check differentiation stability. The rank threshold combines a relative cutoff of $10^{-7}\sigma_{\max}$, disagreement between the two Jacobians, and floating-point precision. For the nonzero model at the 69 Step 7 configurations, the full 34-parameter Jacobian has one numerical-null singular value, $3.96\times10^{-8}$, below the $3.05\times10^{-5}$ threshold. Its rank is therefore 33/34. This near-zero value represents an unobservable direction rather than a positioning error.

The missing direction has a geometric explanation. Rotating the base about the exact first axis while counter-rotating the other axes and tool frame leaves tracker-frame predictions unchanged. This freedom to express identical predictions with different parameters is called \emph{gauge freedom}. Fixing parameter 0, the base-correction twist's $x$ rotation component, selects one local representative. It does not establish that the physical base roll, conventionally rotation about $x$, is zero; the stored twist component is also not generally an Euler roll angle. The constraint must intersect the unobservable direction: if $J_zv=0$ is the sole null direction, fixing coordinate $j$ removes it locally only when $v_j\neq0$. Arbitrarily fixing an unrelated parameter need not resolve the ambiguity.

With parameter 0 fixed, the remaining Jacobian has rank 33/33. Its singular-value extrema are $\sigma_{\max}=305.125$ and $\sigma_{\min}=0.361120$, giving the condition number
\begin{equation*}
\kappa=\frac{\sigma_{\max}}{\sigma_{\min}}=844.94.
\end{equation*}
This ratio describes the disparity between the strongest and weakest sensitivities in the chosen scaling; it is not an error in mm or rad. The weakest observable combinations mix the linear and quadratic joint-1 and joint-3 coefficients, whose positive-only travel intervals make those effects harder to separate. Full numerical rank therefore does not guarantee precise parameter recovery under noise. The target-based residual weights are not a measured noise covariance.

For identical Jacobian blocks repeated $n$ times, singular values scale as $\sqrt{n}$ while rank is unchanged. Independent repeated observations can reduce random noise but cannot supply missing excitation. The single-axis trajectory distinguishes all 33 free directions locally in this audit; mixed-pose tests assess prediction beyond those sweeps. This local result does not prove global uniqueness, meaning the absence of a different solution elsewhere in parameter space, or identifiability of arbitrary joint-distortion functions.

\section{Algorithmic Approach and Implementation}\label{sec:implementation}
\subsection{Workflow and Module Responsibilities}
The notebook orchestrates the public robot/tracker APIs. \texttt{metrics.py} computes relative-frame errors; \path{registration.py} supplies rigid registration and proper frame means; \texttt{workflow.py} handles homing, pose planning, paired observations, and base initialization. \path{kinematics.py} implements corrected FK, residuals, fitting, rank analysis, and physical parameter reports. Full-interval constraints live in the student-owned \path{vendor/functions/_fit_constraints.py}. Supplementary scripts/tests are in \texttt{experiments/}; data, fits, and results are in \texttt{results/}. Protected TA simulation, assertions, and inverse code are unchanged.

\begin{algorithm}[!htbp]
\caption{Calibration and frozen-model evaluation}\label{alg:acquisition}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\State Build the nominal scene and exact calibration marker body.
\State Estimate additive home offsets from five homings.
\State Acquire ten baseline observations for diagnostic alignment.
\State Plan and execute the 765-command single-axis trajectory.
\For{each commanded configuration}
 \State Copy settled public encoder values and acquire one tracker snapshot.
 \If{the calibration-body observation is available}
  \State Store a copied encoder/frame pair.
 \EndIf
\EndFor
\State Average $F_{\mathrm{obs}}F_{\mathrm{nom}}^{-1}$ for base initialization.
\State Fit the constrained model with one gauge coordinate fixed.
\State Save and freeze parameters/data hashes before new truth evaluation.
\State Evaluate tracker prediction, physical FK, own-model consistency, and executed IK separately.
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

Missing observations produce no unmatched pair or cached substitute, but their sampling/motion time remains charged. Reference returns are encoder-zero commands, not additional physical homings. The measurement plan repeats commands, while the collector's \texttt{repeats} option alone repeats snapshots after one move; this distinction matters for robot repeatability noise.

\subsection{Focused Unit Tests and Regression Evidence}
Four high-value test groups are emphasized; other required component tests are still run.
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=2pt]
\item \textbf{Forward-model geometry.} Zero corrections recover nominal FK; noncommuting base/tool perturbations detect multiplication-order mistakes, known tilts check geometry, and random nonzero models maintain $R^TR=I$ and $\det R=1$.
\item \textbf{Fitting and held-out recovery.} Noiseless model-generated data use nonzero parameters, 24 training poses, and 40 new poses. Historical equation-level recovery reaches roughly $10^{-13}$ mm / $10^{-16}$ rad, while feasible constrained fixtures and archived fits verify physical bounds. Tests require meaningful improvement over the initial model, not only a solver success flag. This is numerical consistency, not simulator accuracy.
\item \textbf{Rank, constraints, and acquisition integrity.} Central-difference/SVD checks verify the 33/34 gauge, 33/33 reduced rank, and repetition invariance. Independent polynomial-extremum checks validate complete-interval constraints. Workflow fixtures test home-offset sign, copied synchronized measurements, missing observations, limits, visibility, and exact clock accounting.
\item \textbf{Inverse edge cases and failure accounting.} Own-FK targets provide reachable witnesses for nominal, mixed-wrist, near-pole, and boundary round trips. Strict $10^{-6}$ mm / $10^{-8}$ rad tolerances expose wrapping/convergence failures. Executed-IK fixtures ensure inaccurate returns are still executed/scored and exceptions remain in denominators.
\end{enumerate}
After the explicit Step 4 metric update, the complete regression suite produced \textbf{253 passed, 3 expected failures, and 1 failure} in 32.12 s. The three strict expected failures document retained inverse limitations. The remaining failure is \path{test_refine_marker_body_reduces_layout_error}: optional manufacturing-layout refinement remains unimplemented because the selected special body has exact coordinates. This exception is disclosed; the suite is not described as entirely passing.

Metric validation includes 28 student-owned tests and three unchanged TA metric tests. These cover identity, tiny angles, negative rotations, principal-angle wrapping, near/exact $\pi$, relative-frame composition, and error aggregation. A retained 1064-rotation sweep with random axes and endpoint-scale angles has a recorded maximum absolute angle error of $4.44\times10^{-16}$ rad. The original notebook sanity expression returns $(0.01~\mathrm{rad},1.0~\mathrm{mm})$. These checks validate the metric implementation, not physical robot accuracy.

\subsection{Reproducibility}
Frozen datasets/models and saved per-attempt records permit report generation without reacquisition. Each seed uses its own calibration data and parameters. Geometry uses \path{SeedSequence([20261025, robot seed, category])}; calibration sensor streams are 606, 1607, and 1661, and evaluation streams are 3101, 3107, and 3161. \section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}
\subsection{Accuracy and Physical Plausibility}
The model generalizes from single-axis calibration to mixed continuous poses, meeting FK targets in every tested category and physical IK targets at all random targets. Constraining joint distortion improves plausibility with negligible loss of prediction accuracy. The gauge audit explains why useful predictions do not imply unique base/tool coordinates or precise individual polynomial coefficients. Residual error can combine quadratic approximation error, systematic tracker bias, and robot repeatability; these effects are not uniquely separated by the available data.

Single-observation tracker measurements showed substantially larger orientation errors than deterministic physical FK evaluations, reflecting measurement noise rather than a failure of the calibrated kinematic model.

\subsection{Design Trade-offs and Limitations}
The 69-configuration trajectory excites the selected gauge-fixed model efficiently. Repetitions lower independent noise but neither add rank nor remove bias. The quadratic is compact, yet does not inherit the classroom interpolation bound for sinusoidal distortion. Three finite robot realizations cannot certify arbitrary-seed or continuous worst-case performance, and Seed 1 remains historical development evidence. Good FK or random-target IK accuracy also does not establish strict numerical consistency: the unchanged inverse still fails substantially at limits, boundaries, and poles. Averaged tracker diagnostics must not replace noisy single-observation or physical-placement tests.

\subsection{Smallest Justified Next Improvements}
The first priority is inverse-solver handling: start from the supplied seed with nominal fallbacks, balance angular/translation units, keep iterates feasible and encoders unwrapped, and validate the final FK residual before selecting a solution. These are supported recommendations, not changes applied to the reported solver. If systematic profile residuals later dominate, bounded one-dimensional piecewise-linear corrections would match the classroom interpolation argument. Any extension should use permitted tracker data and new held-out configurations, without tuning to the ground-truth test errors. Noise-aware weighting likewise requires covariance estimation rather than treating accuracy targets as standard deviations.

\Needspace{6\baselineskip}
\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}
A visible tetrahedral-marker setup and approximately 554 s simulated acquisition identify a physically constrained, gauge-fixed kinematic model. Independent calibrations of three robots pass all 3960 supplementary FK configurations and all 240 continuous random executed-IK targets, while preserving the original Seed 1 fixed-protocol successes. Maximum random executed errors are 0.13286 mm and $7.98\times10^{-4}$ rad. However, only 90/180 stress IK targets pass; failures of the unchanged inverse prevent a claim of reliable full-workspace inverse performance. The report therefore separates calibration prediction quality, noisy tracker observations, numerical consistency, and actual placement, and retains the unresolved solver and model limitations.

\subsection{Mathematical Explanation Behind Few Core Implementations}

\textbf{Homing and Base-Frame Initialization}
At physical home, $0=\theta_{\mathrm{home},k}+\delta_{\mathrm{sys},k}+\delta_{\mathrm{rnd},k}$. The additive correction is therefore
\begin{equation}
 o_k=-\frac{1}{5}\sum_{i=1}^{5}\theta_{\mathrm{home},i,k}.
\end{equation}
The negative sign is essential. This correction is applied once in the improved joint map; stored encoder observations remain unchanged. For each paired observation, right-cancel nominal FK:
\begin{equation}
 F_{X,j}=F_{\mathrm{obs},j}\big[F_{BE}^{\mathrm{nom}}(\thv_j)\big]^{-1},
 \qquad F_{X,0}=\operatorname{AverageFrames}\{F_{X,j}\}.
\end{equation}
This preserves the order $F_{TE}=F_XF_{BE}$. With imperfect nominal kinematics, $F_{X,0}$ is a baseline alignment rather than an isolated measurement of the physical base pose. The ten initial mixed poses are diagnostic; the final initialization uses the 765 main calibration pairs.

\textbf{Rigid Frames and Pose-Error Metrics}
A rigid frame $F=(R,p)$ acts on a point as $Rx+p$. Composition and inversion follow
\begin{equation}
(R_1,p_1)(R_2,p_2)=(R_1R_2,R_1p_2+p_1),\qquad
F^{-1}=(R^T,-R^Tp).
\end{equation}
Following the classroom relative-frame convention, define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pose_error}
\Delta F=F_{\mathrm{ref}}^{-1}F_{\mathrm{pred}}=(\Delta R,\Delta p),\qquad
e_R=\|\operatorname{Log}_{SO(3)}(\Delta R)\|_2,\quad e_p=\|\Delta p\|_2.
\end{equation}
The principal rotation angle is in radians and translation error is in millimeters. Since $\Delta p=R_{\mathrm{ref}}^T(p_{\mathrm{pred}}-p_{\mathrm{ref}})$, its norm equals Euclidean position error. This is the translation of the relative frame, not the translational part of an $SE(3)$ logarithm.

The scalar angle is computed explicitly from Rodrigues' formula, which gives $\Delta R-\Delta R^T=2\sin\theta\,\operatorname{sk}(n)$ and $\operatorname{tr}(\Delta R)=1+2\cos\theta$. Thus,
\begin{equation*}
v=\frac12
\begin{bmatrix}
\Delta R_{32}-\Delta R_{23}\\
\Delta R_{13}-\Delta R_{31}\\
\Delta R_{21}-\Delta R_{12}
\end{bmatrix},
\qquad
c=\operatorname{clip}\!\left(
\frac{\operatorname{tr}(\Delta R)-1}{2},-1,1\right),
\qquad
e_R=\operatorname{atan2}(\|v\|_2,c).
\end{equation*}
For a valid rotation this returns the exact principal angle in $[0,\pi]$, without quaternion conversion or a small-angle approximation. Using both sine and cosine avoids the small-angle cancellation of trace--arccos extraction. Clipping handles floating-point endpoint drift; it does not repair an invalid rotation matrix. The fitting objective retains the signed rotation-vector residuals in \cref{eq:least_squares}. For $N$ scored poses, RMS is $\sqrt{N^{-1}\sum_i e_i^2}$, separately for each unit; maxima and percentiles are also retained
