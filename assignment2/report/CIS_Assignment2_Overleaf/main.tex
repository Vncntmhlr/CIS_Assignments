\section*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Abstract}
A tracker-based calibration workflow is developed for a Cartesian robot with a spherical wrist. A tetrahedral marker body and nominal visibility envelope guide scene design. Five homings and 765 observations across 69 single-axis configurations initialize a 34-parameter geometric and quadratic joint-distortion model. A fixed gauge leaves 33 optimized parameters; full-interval magnitude and derivative constraints enforce known joint-error bounds. Independent calibrations of seeds 1, 7, and 61 each require approximately 554 s simulated acquisition and 64 s wall-clock fitting. The preserved Seed 1 protocols pass 729/729 FK configurations and 40/40 physical IK targets. Supplementary testing passes 3960/3960 physical FK configurations and 240/240 random executed IK targets, with respective overall maxima of 0.12485 mm / $4.98\times10^{-4}$ rad and 0.13286 mm / $7.98\times10^{-4}$ rad. Stress tests expose substantial limitations of the unchanged provided inverse: only 90/180 targets pass both accuracy criteria. Tracker noise, gauge-dependent parameter recovery, and finite workspace coverage remain explicit limitations.

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:introduction}
\subsection{Background and Problem Statement}
Accurate robot positioning requires a kinematic model that accounts for departures from nominal geometry and encoder readings. This assignment calibrates a Cartesian robot with three prismatic joints and a three-axis spherical wrist, using an optical tracker and an end-effector calibration body. Base-placement error, axis misalignment, tool-frame error, and systematic joint distortion cause the physical robot to depart from its nominal model. External tracker observations provide the measurements needed to estimate these corrections.

\subsection{Objectives and Performance Requirements}
The assignment asks for improved forward and inverse kinematics over the work volume, with the performance requirements summarized in \cref{tab:requirements} \parencite{cisi_assignment2}.
\begin{table}[!htbp]\centering
\caption{Accuracy and execution requirements from the Assignment 2 handout.}\label{tab:requirements}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lll@{}}\toprule
Criterion & Required bound & Evaluation note\\\midrule
Position accuracy (FK and IK) & \SI{0.2}{\milli\metre} & Workspace-level evaluation\\
Orientation accuracy (FK and IK) & \SI{1e-3}{\radian} & Workspace-level evaluation\\
Program execution & \SI{10}{\minute} & Instructor runtime limit\\\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}
RMS and maximum position/orientation errors are reported separately, together with the fraction of attempted targets meeting both accuracy bounds. An acceptable average does not establish accuracy at every pose. Simulated acquisition/testing time and wall-clock computation time are recorded separately; the ten-minute program-execution limit must not be confused with the simulator's elapsed-time counter.

\subsection{Summary of the Proposed Approach}
We follow the recommended parameter-estimation route (Method~1): repeated homing establishes joint offsets, individual joint sweeps supply calibration observations, and physically constrained nonlinear least squares fits a corrected geometric model with smooth joint-distortion terms. This avoids a dense six-dimensional calibration grid. Evaluation distinguishes tracker prediction, physical FK accuracy, numerical FK/IK consistency, and executed physical IK accuracy. Seeds 1, 7, and 61 are calibrated independently, with ordinary-workspace and stress-test results kept separate. Hidden simulator values are used only for evaluation; the provided inverse solver is retained so its numerical limitations can be distinguished from forward-model accuracy.

The tracker has a square viewing frustum extending from \SI{1000}{\milli\metre} to \SI{2200}{\milli\metre} along its local $+z$ axis. Marker measurements include independent per-coordinate noise with standard deviation \SI{0.1}{\milli\metre} and spatially varying systematic error. Camera-pose jiggle is disabled by the assignment, which does not make the marker observations noiseless. Each snapshot costs \SI{0.03}{\second} of simulated time.

\subsection{Coordinate Frames and Nominal Geometry}
The nominal robot model is expressed as \parencite{cisi_assignment2,cisi_frames}:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:nominal_fk}
F_{BE}^{\mathrm{nom}}(\thv)
=F_c(\theta_1,\theta_2,\theta_3)\,F_{cw}\,F_w(\theta_4,\theta_5,\theta_6)\,F_{we}.
\end{equation}
Let $W$, $T$, $B$, and $E$ denote workspace, tracker, robot-base, and end-effector frames. The convention $F_{AB}={}^AT_B$ maps coordinates from $B$ into $A$. In \cref{eq:nominal_fk}, $F_c=\operatorname{Trans}(\theta_1,\theta_2,\theta_3)$, $F_{cw}=\operatorname{Trans}(500,0,0)$, and $F_{we}=\operatorname{Trans}(100,0,0)$, with translations in millimeters; $F_w$ applies the ordered rotations $R_x(\theta_4)R_y(\theta_5)R_z(\theta_6)$. Rotations are embedded in homogeneous transformations. The tracker-frame prediction is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tracker_chain}
\widehat F_{TE}(\thv)=\widehat F_{TB}\,F_{BE}(\thv),
\qquad F_{TB}=F_{WT}^{-1}F_{WB}.
\end{equation}
The estimated base-to-tracker transform is applied once on the left; the nominal or corrected $F_{BE}$ describes the robot's internal kinematics.

\subsection{Calibration Marker Body Design}
Four markers form a regular tetrahedron centered at the end-effector origin, with coordinates given in \cref{tab:markers}. Each marker has radius $37.5\sqrt{3}=\SI{64.95}{\milli\metre}$, below the \SI{75}{\milli\metre} limit. This symmetric, noncoplanar layout spreads the observations in three dimensions and avoids favoring one rotational direction. The special calibration body's geometry is exact under the TA API, and its frame coincides with $E$. Therefore, no marker-manufacturing refinement or separate marker-to-tool transform is estimated.

\subsection{Camera Placement and Visibility Analysis}
The tracker frame coincides with the workspace frame and looks along $+z$. The chosen approximate robot-base pose is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:scene_placement}
F_{WT}=I,\qquad F_{WB}^{\mathrm{approx}}=\operatorname{Trans}(-800,0,1300),
\end{equation}
with translation coordinates in millimeters. Including the fixed base-to-wrist offset, nominal wrist-center travel is $x,y\in[-300,300]$ mm and $z\in[1300,1900]$ mm, centered at $(0,0,1600)$ mm.

For any wrist orientation, the tool length and marker radius bound the distance from wrist center to a marker by $\rho=100+64.95=\SI{164.95}{\milli\metre}$. Thus a conservative marker envelope is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:marker_envelope}
|x|,|y|\leq\SI{464.95}{\milli\metre},\qquad
z\in[1135.05,2064.95]\ \mathrm{mm}.
\end{equation}
The frustum half-width is $h(z)=500+(500/1200)(z-1000)$, with $z$ and $h$ in millimeters. At the envelope's nearest depth, $h=\SI{556.27}{\milli\metre}$, giving at least \SI{91.32}{\milli\metre} lateral clearance and approximately \SI{135.05}{\milli\metre} near/far clearance. This bound covers the complete nominal joint range, including rotations between sampled poses. It is a nominal geometric result, not a guarantee under every unknown physical perturbation.

\subsection{Design Verification and Unit Tests}
Design checks require all markers to satisfy the radius constraint, the centered layout to span three dimensions, every planned command to satisfy joint limits, and every nominal marker to remain in the tracker frustum. All 81 initial visualization poses pass visibility checks. An additional sweep using three values for each linear joint and five for each wrist joint retains all markers at $3^3\times5^3=3375/3375$ nominal poses. These numerical checks support the analytic envelope; they do not establish calibration accuracy.

The acquisition setup uses five homings, ten mixed baseline observations, and 765 calibration observations across 69 distinct single-axis configurations. Eleven re-commanded readings per configuration, plus return-reference readings, renew both tracker and robot repeatability noise. Each valid tracker frame is paired with its settled encoder reading; missing observations are not replaced by stale data. The same design is applied independently to each seed, costing approximately \SIrange{554.03}{554.06}{\second} simulated time including homing and baseline measurements. Sampling-density, repetition, and estimator derivations are developed in \cref{sec:math}.

\section{Mathematical Methods}\label{sec:math}
\subsection{Rigid Frames and Pose-Error Metrics}
A rigid frame $F=(R,p)$ acts on a point as $Rx+p$. Composition and inversion follow
\begin{equation}
(R_1,p_1)(R_2,p_2)=(R_1R_2,R_1p_2+p_1),\qquad
F^{-1}=(R^T,-R^Tp).
\end{equation}
Following the classroom relative-frame convention \parencite[slides~38,45]{cisi_frames}, define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pose_error}
\Delta F=F_{\mathrm{ref}}^{-1}F_{\mathrm{pred}}=(\Delta R,\Delta p),\qquad
e_R=\|\operatorname{Log}_{SO(3)}(\Delta R)\|_2,\quad e_p=\|\Delta p\|_2.
\end{equation}
The principal rotation angle is in radians and translation error is in millimeters. Since $\Delta p=R_{\mathrm{ref}}^T(p_{\mathrm{pred}}-p_{\mathrm{ref}})$, its norm equals Euclidean position error. This is the translation of the relative frame, not the translational part of an $SE(3)$ logarithm. Quaternion-based angle extraction avoids the small-angle cancellation of a trace--arccos formula. For $N$ scored poses, RMS is $\sqrt{N^{-1}\sum_i e_i^2}$, separately for each unit; maxima and percentiles are also retained.

\subsection{Rigid Registration and Averaging of Frames}
Given corresponding local and measured marker points $a_k,b_k$, Arun/SVD registration minimizes $\sum_k\|Ra_k+t-b_k\|^2$ over $R\in\SO$ \parencite[slide~17]{cisi_registration}. With centered row-coordinate matrices $A_c,B_c$,
\begin{equation}
H=A_c^TB_c=U\Sigma V^T,\quad
R=V\operatorname{diag}(1,1,\det(VU^T))U^T,\quad t=\bar b-R\bar a.
\end{equation}
The determinant correction excludes reflections. At least three noncollinear correspondences are required; numerical rank below two is rejected. Coplanar noncollinear points can still determine a proper rigid transform.

To average repeated frames, translations are averaged in their common target frame. For $M=N^{-1}\sum_jR_j=U\Sigma V^T$, use
\begin{equation}
\bar R=U\operatorname{diag}(1,1,\det(UV^T))V^T.
\end{equation}
This is the least-squares projection of the matrix mean onto $SO(3)$: orthogonality is preserved and the determinant is $+1$. It is a chordal rotation mean, not an average of Euler angles or an intrinsic $SE(3)$ mean. Equal weighting does not remove fixed tracker bias.

\subsection{Homing and Base-Frame Initialization}
At physical home, $0=\theta_{\mathrm{home},k}+\delta_{\mathrm{sys},k}+\delta_{\mathrm{rnd},k}$. The additive correction is therefore
\begin{equation}
 o_k=-\frac{1}{5}\sum_{i=1}^{5}\theta_{\mathrm{home},i,k}.
\end{equation}
The negative sign is essential. This correction is applied once in the improved joint map; stored encoder observations remain unchanged. For each paired observation, right-cancel nominal FK:
\begin{equation}
 F_{X,j}=F_{\mathrm{obs},j}\big[F_{BE}^{\mathrm{nom}}(\thv_j)\big]^{-1},
 \qquad F_{X,0}=\operatorname{AverageFrames}\{F_{X,j}\}.
\end{equation}
This preserves the order $F_{TE}=F_XF_{BE}$. With imperfect nominal kinematics, $F_{X,0}$ is a baseline alignment rather than an isolated measurement of the physical base pose. The ten initial mixed poses are diagnostic; the final initialization uses the 765 main calibration pairs.

\subsection{Sampling Density and Repeated Measurements}
The classroom interpolation bound for a one-dimensional error profile with $\|e'\|\leq L$ is \parencite{cisi_interpolation}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:interp}
 \|e_{\mathrm{interp}}\|\leq Lh/2.
\end{equation}
For a fixed-wrist linear sweep, a conservative combined robot/tracker slope is
\begin{equation}
L=\omega_{\mathrm{rob}}+\omega_{\mathrm{trk}}(1+\omega_{\mathrm{rob}})
=0.001250375\ \mathrm{mm/mm}.
\end{equation}
An engineering allocation of 0.025 mm per profile gives $h\leq39.99$ mm; 17 levels over 600 mm provide 37.5 mm spacing. For wrist joint distortion, $\omega_{\mathrm{rob}}=5\times10^{-5}$ rad/rad and a $0.001/24$ rad allocation permits $h\leq1.667$ rad. A tighter $\pi/4$ observability cap supplies several angular levels: 7, 7, and 9 for joints 4--6. These are profile-spacing arguments, not a proof of global pose accuracy; in particular, a global quadratic does not inherit a piecewise-linear interpolation guarantee.

For centered markers define $G=\sum_k(\|a_k\|^2I-a_ka_k^T)$. The tetrahedron gives $G=11250I$ mm$^2$. Linearized independent marker noise implies $\operatorname{Cov}(\alphav)\simeq\sigma_b^2G^{-1}$. Adding nominal independent wrist repeatability gives an angular RMS estimate
\begin{equation}
\sigma_R\simeq\sqrt{3(0.1)^2/11250+3(10^{-4})^2}=0.001642\ \mathrm{rad}.
\end{equation}
Allocating half the angular target to random RMS yields $n=\lceil(0.001642/0.0005)^2\rceil=11$ re-commanded readings. The plan has 69 unique configurations, 11 observations each, and six reference returns: $69\times11+6=765$. Re-commanding renews robot noise, unlike repeated snapshots after a single move. Independence and small-error linearization are engineering approximations; systematic error and model mismatch do not average away.

\subsection{Corrected Kinematic Model}
The stored vector retains 34 parameters, summarized in \cref{tab:parameter_layout}. Each imperfect axis has two perpendicular tilt components; rotation along its own axis is excluded. The first prismatic axis remains exact. Exact rotation exponentials preserve $SO(3)$ rather than treating $I+\operatorname{sk}(\phi)$ as a finite rotation.
\begin{table}[!htbp]\centering\small
\caption{Parameter layout for the quadratic model.}\label{tab:parameter_layout}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lll@{}}\toprule
Indices (zero-based) & Meaning & Count\\\midrule
$0{:}6$ & Base correction twist, rotation then translation & 6\\
$6{:}16$ & Two perpendicular tilts for axes 2--6 & 10\\
$16{:}22$ & Wrist-to-tool correction twist & 6\\
$22{:}34$ & Linear/quadratic coefficients for six joints & 12\\\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}
With fixed home correction $o_k$, normalization $s_k=\max(|\theta_{\min,k}|,|\theta_{\max,k}|)$, and $x_k=(\theta_k+o_k)/s_k$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:joint_map}
\delta_k(\theta_k)=o_k+c_{k1}x_k+c_{k2}x_k^2,\qquad
q_k=\theta_k+\delta_k(\theta_k).
\end{equation}
The variable polynomial terms vanish at the measured home encoder $\theta_k=-o_k$, giving $q_k(-o_k)=0$; home offsets are not counted twice. Coefficients have the joint's units. The transform chain is
\begin{align}
F_{BE}(\thv)&=\operatorname{Trans}\!\left(\sum_{k=1}^{3}q_kd'_k\right)F_{cw}
 \prod_{k=4}^{6}\operatorname{Rot}(n'_k,q_k)\,F_{we}\operatorname{Exp}_{SE(3)}(\xi_{we}),\\
F_X&=F_{X,0}\operatorname{Exp}_{SE(3)}(\xi_X).
\end{align}
Axis tilts rotate the nominal axes. Base/tool twists use rotation-first ordering; their actual translations are $J(\alpha)\epsilon$, not simply the translational twist coordinates.

\subsection{Physically Constrained Nonlinear Fitting}
For each observed/predicted pair, retain signed components:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:least_squares}
r_j(\betav)=\begin{bmatrix}
\operatorname{Log}_{SO(3)}(\Delta R_j)/0.001\\
\Delta p_j/0.2
\end{bmatrix},\qquad
\widehat\betav=\arg\min_{\betav\in\mathcal C}\frac12\sum_{j=1}^{765}\|r_j(\betav)\|^2.
\end{equation}
These target-based weights are dimensionless, not covariance whitening. Scalar error magnitudes alone would discard directional information. All 765 observations enter the objective individually; no averaging or truth-based tuning is used in fitting.

Joint-error specifications impose, throughout each permitted encoder interval,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:physical_constraints}
 |\delta_k(\theta)|\leq D_k,\qquad
 |\delta'_k(\theta)|\leq\Omega_k,
\end{equation}
For joints 1--3, $D_k=0.2$ mm and $\Omega_k=5\times10^{-4}$; for joints 4--6, $D_k=0.01$ rad and $\Omega_k=5\times10^{-5}$. Quadratic magnitude extrema occur at interval endpoints or an interior stationary point; the derivative is linear, so its extrema occur at the endpoints. Constraints are therefore checked over the full interval analytically, including the home anchor.

Following the classroom parameter-estimation framework \parencite{cisi_calibration,cisi_leastsquares}, optimization uses dimensionless parameter scales: 0.01 rad for angular corrections, 1 mm for base translation, 0.5 mm for tool translation, and 0.2 mm / 0.01 rad for linear/angular distortion coefficients. An initial residual-Jacobian SVD further preconditions the 33 free coordinates. Scaled SLSQP uses $\mathrm{ftol}=10^{-10}$ and a 100-evaluation/iteration budget, with central numerical residual derivatives and analytic constraint gradients. Dividing the objective by sample count changes scaling, not its minimizer. The parameter vector is updated only after successful, finite, physically feasible optimization. Historical unconstrained trust-region fits remain separate comparisons.

\subsection{Identifiability and Gauge Freedom}
The actual signed weighted residual Jacobian is differentiated at scaled steps $10^{-4}\max(1,|\beta_i/S_i|)$ and twice that step. SVD rank uses a relative cutoff together with finite-difference disagreement. In the nonzero model audit using the 69 Step 7 configurations, the full Jacobian has rank 33/34. Fixing parameter 0 (base-twist $x$ rotation) gives rank 33/33, singular extrema 305.125 and 0.361120, and condition number 844.94. The full numerical null singular value is $3.96\times10^{-8}$, below the $3.05\times10^{-5}$ rank threshold.

Physically, a base rotation about the exact first axis can be counteracted by rotating the other axes and tool frame, leaving tracker-frame predictions unchanged. Fixing one coordinate selects a gauge; it does not measure true base roll. The weakest observable modes mix the linear and quadratic joint-1 and joint-3 coefficients, especially because their travel intervals are positive-only. Thus full reduced numerical rank does not imply precise physical parameter recovery under noise.

Repeating the same configuration stacks equivalent Jacobian blocks: singular values increase as $\sqrt n$, but rank and missing excitation do not improve. Single-axis sweeps collectively excite the selected model after gauge fixing; their adequacy is tested further on mixed configurations. This local audit is not a proof of global uniqueness or identifiability of arbitrary distortion functions.

\subsection{Provided Inverse Kinematics}
The unchanged inverse uses nominal wrist branches as initial guesses, finite-difference Jacobians, and damped Gauss--Newton updates for at most 50 iterations. It accepts in-limit candidates and chooses the one nearest the caller's seed. The seed guides selection rather than necessarily providing an optimization start. Residuals mix raw radians and millimeters, and a returned candidate is not independently gated by a final pose-accuracy check.

Near wrist poles, the joint-to-pose Jacobian loses rank. Exact limits also expose numerical feasibility sensitivity. Furthermore, post-solve angle wrapping can alter FK because the calibrated polynomial is nonperiodic:
\begin{equation}
q_k(\theta+2\pi)-q_k(\theta)=2\pi+\delta_k(\theta+2\pi)-\delta_k(\theta).
\end{equation}
These issues are evaluated honestly, without replacing the provided solver.

\section{Algorithmic Approach and Implementation}\label{sec:implementation}
\subsection{Workflow and Module Responsibilities}
The notebook orchestrates the public robot/tracker APIs. \texttt{metrics.py} computes relative-frame errors; \path{registration.py} supplies rigid registration and proper frame means; \texttt{workflow.py} handles homing, pose planning, paired observations, and base initialization. \path{kinematics.py} implements corrected FK, residuals, fitting, rank analysis, and physical parameter reports. Full-interval constraints live in the student-owned \path{vendor/functions/_fit_constraints.py}. Supplementary scripts/tests are in \texttt{experiments/}; data, fits, and results are in \texttt{results/}. Protected TA simulation, assertions, and inverse code are unchanged.

\begin{algorithm}[!htbp]
\caption{Calibration and frozen-model evaluation}\label{alg:acquisition}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\State Build the nominal scene and exact calibration marker body.
\State Estimate additive home offsets from five homings.
\State Acquire ten baseline observations for diagnostic alignment.
\State Plan and execute the 765-command single-axis trajectory.
\For{each commanded configuration}
 \State Copy settled public encoder values and acquire one tracker snapshot.
 \If{the calibration-body observation is available}
  \State Store a copied encoder/frame pair.
 \EndIf
\EndFor
\State Average $F_{\mathrm{obs}}F_{\mathrm{nom}}^{-1}$ for base initialization.
\State Fit the constrained model with one gauge coordinate fixed.
\State Save and freeze parameters/data hashes before new truth evaluation.
\State Evaluate tracker prediction, physical FK, own-model consistency, and executed IK separately.
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

Missing observations produce no unmatched pair or cached substitute, but their sampling/motion time remains charged. Reference returns are encoder-zero commands, not additional physical homings. The measurement plan repeats commands, while the collector's \texttt{repeats} option alone repeats snapshots after one move; this distinction matters for robot repeatability noise.

\subsection{Focused Unit Tests and Regression Evidence}
Four high-value test groups are emphasized; other required component tests are still run.
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=2pt]
\item \textbf{Forward-model geometry.} Zero corrections recover nominal FK; noncommuting base/tool perturbations detect multiplication-order mistakes, known tilts check geometry, and random nonzero models maintain $R^TR=I$ and $\det R=1$.
\item \textbf{Fitting and held-out recovery.} Noiseless model-generated data use nonzero parameters, 24 training poses, and 40 new poses. Historical equation-level recovery reaches roughly $10^{-13}$ mm / $10^{-16}$ rad, while feasible constrained fixtures and archived fits verify physical bounds. Tests require meaningful improvement over the initial model, not only a solver success flag. This is numerical consistency, not simulator accuracy.
\item \textbf{Rank, constraints, and acquisition integrity.} Central-difference/SVD checks verify the 33/34 gauge, 33/33 reduced rank, and repetition invariance. Independent polynomial-extremum checks validate complete-interval constraints. Workflow fixtures test home-offset sign, copied synchronized measurements, missing observations, limits, visibility, and exact clock accounting.
\item \textbf{Inverse edge cases and failure accounting.} Own-FK targets provide reachable witnesses for nominal, mixed-wrist, near-pole, and boundary round trips. Strict $10^{-6}$ mm / $10^{-8}$ rad tolerances expose wrapping/convergence failures. Executed-IK fixtures ensure inaccurate returns are still executed/scored and exceptions remain in denominators.
\end{enumerate}
The full regression run for this report produced \textbf{252 passed, 3 expected failures, and 1 failure} in 31.06 s. The three strict expected failures document retained inverse limitations. The remaining failure is \path{test_refine_marker_body_reduces_layout_error}: optional manufacturing-layout refinement remains unimplemented because the selected special body has exact coordinates. This exception is disclosed; the suite is not described as entirely passing.

\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}
\subsection{Accuracy and Physical Plausibility}
The model generalizes from single-axis calibration to mixed continuous poses, meeting FK targets in every tested category and physical IK targets at all random targets. Constraining joint distortion improves plausibility with negligible loss of prediction accuracy. The gauge audit explains why useful predictions do not imply unique base/tool coordinates or precise individual polynomial coefficients. Residual error can combine quadratic approximation error, systematic tracker bias, and robot repeatability; these effects are not uniquely separated by the available data.

\subsection{Design Trade-offs and Limitations}
The 69-configuration trajectory excites the selected gauge-fixed model efficiently. Repetitions lower independent noise but neither add rank nor remove bias. The quadratic is compact, yet does not inherit the classroom interpolation bound for sinusoidal distortion. Three finite robot realizations cannot certify arbitrary-seed or continuous worst-case performance, and Seed 1 remains historical development evidence. Good FK or random-target IK accuracy also does not establish strict numerical consistency: the unchanged inverse still fails substantially at limits, boundaries, and poles. Averaged tracker diagnostics must not replace noisy single-observation or physical-placement tests.

\subsection{Smallest Justified Next Improvements}
The first priority is inverse-solver handling: start from the supplied seed with nominal fallbacks, balance angular/translation units, keep iterates feasible and encoders unwrapped, and validate the final FK residual before selecting a solution. These are supported recommendations, not changes applied to the reported solver. If systematic profile residuals later dominate, bounded one-dimensional piecewise-linear corrections would match the classroom interpolation argument. Any extension should use permitted tracker data and new held-out configurations, without tuning to the ground-truth test errors. Noise-aware weighting likewise requires covariance estimation rather than treating accuracy targets as standard deviations.

\Needspace{6\baselineskip}
\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}
A visible tetrahedral-marker setup and approximately 554 s simulated acquisition identify a physically constrained, gauge-fixed kinematic model. Independent calibrations of three robots pass all 3960 supplementary FK configurations and all 240 continuous random executed-IK targets, while preserving the original Seed 1 fixed-protocol successes. Maximum random executed errors are 0.13286 mm and $7.98\times10^{-4}$ rad. However, only 90/180 stress IK targets pass; failures of the unchanged inverse prevent a claim of reliable full-workspace inverse performance. The report therefore separates calibration prediction quality, noisy tracker observations, numerical consistency, and actual placement, and retains the unresolved solver and model limitations.
