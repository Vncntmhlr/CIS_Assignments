## Mathematical Derivations and Key Equations
- In tracker coordinates, the nominal wrist center spans `x,y in [-300,300]` and `z in [1300,1900]` mm. This includes the nominal 500 mm base-to-wrist offset.
- The wrist-to-EE translation has length 100 mm. For any wrist rotation, every marker is within `rho = 100 + 64.952 = 164.952` mm of the wrist center by the triangle inequality. Hence a conservative marker envelope is `x,y in [-464.952,464.952]`, `z in [1135.048,2064.952]` mm. This is a bound, not the exact reachable volume.
- Camera half-width is `h(z) = 500 + (z-1000)*500/1200` mm for `1000 <= z <= 2200`. At the envelope's nearest depth, `h=556.270` mm exceeds its maximum lateral magnitude by approximately 91.3 mm. Near/far depth margins are approximately 135.0 mm. Thus the entire nominal envelope is inside the frustum, including wrist rotations between sampled poses.
- For kinematic round trips, compare `F_ref = FwdKins(theta)` with `F_rec = FwdKins(InvKins(F_ref))` using `e_p = ||p_ref-p_rec||` (mm) and `e_R = ||Log(R_ref^T R_rec)||` (rad). Near a wrist singularity, joint angles need not be unique: reconstruction of the pose is the appropriate correctness criterion.

### Step 4: relative-frame error and angle extraction

- **[COURSE]** Use the TA ordering `deltaF = F_a^-1 F_b`: `deltaR = R_a^T R_b`, `epsilon = R_a^T(p_b-p_a)`. Report the principal axis-angle magnitude `theta in [0,pi]` in radians and `||epsilon||` in millimeters. Since `R_a` is orthogonal, `||epsilon|| = ||p_b-p_a||`. This is the translation of the relative Frame, not the translational component of a full SE(3) logarithm.
- **[COURSE]** Lecture 2, slide 45 defines the relative rotation and axis-angle error, with `deltaR ~= I + sk(alpha)` and `theta ~= ||alpha||` for small error. Its Cayley option uses `a = tan(theta/2)n`, so `theta = 2 atan(||a||)`; the Cayley vector is not the rotation vector `alpha=theta*n`.
- **[DERIVED]** For the unit quaternion convention on Lecture 3, slide 20, `q=[w,v]=[cos(theta/2), n sin(theta/2)]`, the principal angle is `theta = 2 atan2(||v||, |w|)`. Slides 23–24 connect matrix-to-quaternion conversion with axis-angle extraction. The scalar/vector ratio printed in the local slide 24 appears reversed relative to slide 20's definition; the expression here satisfies the identity and 180-degree limits.
- **[ENGINEERING]** Select the quaternion route using `Rotation.from_matrix(deltaR).magnitude()`, preserving the classroom error definition while evaluating the exact principal angle. Cayley requires solving with `I+deltaR`, which is singular at pi. The Rodrigues-derived trace formula `acos(clip((tr(deltaR)-1)/2,-1,1))` is mathematically correct but loses very small angles through cancellation. A first-order skew approximation alone returns `|sin(theta)|`, not the angle for general rotations.
- For `N` error pairs, compute angular and positional RMS separately: `rms_angle=sqrt(sum(theta_i^2)/N)`, `rms_pos=sqrt(sum(d_i^2)/N)`, plus the respective maxima and sample count. Do not combine radians and millimeters into an unscaled 6D norm.

Sources: [classroom-method reference](CIS_I_Assignment2_Classroom_Math_Methods.md), Lecture 2 slides 38/45 and Lecture 3 slides 20/23–24; [SciPy magnitude API](https://docs.scipy.org/doc/scipy/reference/generated/scipy.spatial.transform.Rotation.magnitude.html).

### Step 5: rigid registration and frame averaging

- **[COURSE]** Arun registration (Lecture 3, slide 17; [local method reference, §2.2](CIS_I_Assignment2_Classroom_Math_Methods.md#22-preferred-rigid-3-d3-d-solution-centering--svd-arun-slide-17)): minimize `sum ||R a_i+t-b_i||^2` over `R in SO(3)`. Center both clouds, set `H=A_centered^T B_centered=U S V^T`, then `R=V D U^T`, `t=b_bar-R a_bar`. **[DERIVED]** `D=diag(1,1,sign(det(V U^T)))` corrects reflections along the smallest-singular-value direction, yielding the best proper rigid fit without scaling.
- Require matching finite point sets with at least three noncollinear correspondences. Centered clouds and cross-covariance must have numerical rank at least two; three noncollinear or coplanar markers remain valid. Rank checks use relative floating-point tolerances, not a fixed length threshold. A reflected full-rank cloud generally has nonzero residual under any proper rigid transform; the solver must not hide that discrepancy by returning a reflection.
- **[DERIVED]** Equal-weight chordal frame mean: `p_bar=mean(p_i)`; project `M=mean(R_i)=U S V^T` onto `SO(3)` with `R_bar=U diag(1,1,sign(det(U V^T))) V^T`. This minimizes `sum ||R-R_i||_F^2`, since expanding the objective leaves maximizing `tr(R^T M)`. Orthogonality of `U,V,D` gives `R_bar^T R_bar=I`, and determinant correction gives `det(R_bar)=+1`. This extends the course's SVD constrained least-squares idea; it is a chordal mean, not an intrinsic/geodesic mean or an Euler-angle average.
- **Design decision:** leave `refine_marker_body` unimplemented for now. The special EE calibration body's local layout is exact (`a*_k=a_k`) and its frame is exactly `F_ee`, as stated in notebook Step 5 and [the calibration-body API](src/simulation/calibration_body.py). Re-estimating that known layout from noisy readings would introduce error. Refinement is relevant only if future designs add ordinary bodies with manufacturing uncertainty; repeated pose measurements can still reduce random observation noise for the exact body.

### Step 7: Method 1 measurement planning

- **Official requirements:** Method 1 in the [brief](2026_assignment_2.pdf), p. 6, separates axis/parameter identification from dense workspace mapping. Page 7 limits **program execution to 10 minutes**; this is a wall-clock limit, distinct from simulated acquisition time. - **Trajectory:** common zero-encoder reference; full single-axis sweeps in joints 1–6, holding the other five at zero and returning to the reference after every axis. Step 6 performs physical homing; the pose-list API cannot issue `HomeRobot()` actions, so subsequent returns are nominal zero commands. Linear axes use 17 levels each (37.5 mm spacing); wrist axes 4–5 use seven levels each, axis 6 nine (maximum spacing `pi/4`). Shared zero gives 69 distinct configurations. No 6-D grid or extra mixed poses are required by the prospective rank check below.
- **[DERIVED bound]** Classroom §4.2: exact-endpoint piecewise-linear interpolation of a profile with `||f'||<=L` has error `<=L*h/2`. For each fixed-wrist linear residual profile *after removing the rigid axis model*, `L <= omega_rob + omega_trk*(1+omega_rob) = 0.001250375 mm/mm`. This follows by composing the tracker field with a unit-axis motion whose speed is at most `1+omega_rob`. An **engineering** per-profile allocation of `0.2/8=0.025 mm` gives `h<=39.988 mm`; uniform coverage of 600 mm gives 16 intervals of 37.5 mm, with profile interpolation bound `0.023445 mm`. It bounds these profiles, not arbitrary workspace-dependent tracker bias or final FK error; noisy knots add their own error.
- For wrist *joint distortion alone*, `omega_rob=5e-5 rad/rad`. Allocating `desired_accuracy/24` per joint gives `h<=1.6667 rad` at the default target; an **engineering observability cap** of `pi/4` supplies several well-separated orientation samples. Actual per-joint interpolation bound is `1.9635e-5 rad`, or `5.8905e-5 rad` summed over three joints; a 100 mm tool lever turns the latter into approximately `0.0059 mm`. The amplitude limits (`0.2 mm` linear, `0.01 rad` wrist) show offsets cannot be ignored; slope bounds set spacing. No second-derivative or sinusoidal-frequency assumption is inferred from amplitude bounds, and quadratic fitting is not guaranteed to reproduce every allowed distortion.
- **[DERIVED approximation]** Center markers at `a_bar` and define `G=sum(||a_i-a_bar||^2 I-(a_i-a_bar)(a_i-a_bar)^T)`. Isotropic per-marker noise gives small-error `Cov(alpha) ~= sigma_b^2 G^-1`. Here `G=11250 I mm^2`; marker-only angular RMS is `sqrt(tr(Cov))=0.00163299 rad`. Adding independent wrist motion repeatability gives `sigma_angle~=0.00164215 rad`. Allocate half the requested `0.001 rad` target to random RMS: `N=ceil(sigma_angle^2/(0.0005)^2)=11`. The corresponding position random-RMS estimate, including centroid noise, prismatic noise, and a 100.5 mm wrist lever, is about `0.0268 mm` after 11 re-commanded readings. These are first-order independent-noise estimates, not Gaussian worst-case bounds or fitted-model confidence intervals.
- **Repetitions and efficiency:** each distinct pose receives 11 consecutive re-commanded readings, so both motion and tracker random errors are renewed. Pool the shared reference: initial 11 readings plus one at each of six returns. Total `69*11+6=765` commands/readings. Monotone endpoint traversal minimizes travel within the chosen return-to-reference single-axis family: each joint travels exactly twice its range. This is not a proof of a global time optimum among all possible calibration designs. Smaller angular requests tighten spacing and increase repetitions; geometry determines repetitions. Optional `position_accuracy`, `noise_fraction`, and `max_angular_step` configure these allocations without changing the TA cell.

### Step 8 Phase B: signed residuals and scaled nonlinear least squares

- **[COURSE/DERIVED]** For each paired measurement, form `dF=F_observed^-1 F_predicted`; use `r_j=[Log_SO3(dR)/0.001; dp/0.2]`, with three signed axis-angle components in radians and three signed relative-frame translation components in mm. The translation is `dF.p`, not the translational component of `Log_SE3`. Rotation-vector extraction reuses SciPy's stable matrix/quaternion conversion, consistent with Step 4's principal angle. Scalar error norms would discard direction and are unsuitable optimization residuals.
- **Weighting [ENGINEERING]:** divide by the official angular/position targets, producing dimensionless residuals so a target-sized error in either modality contributes comparably. These scales are not observation standard deviations; this is target-weighted least squares, not covariance whitening or a guaranteed maximum-error constraint. Reuse the Phase A forward/base methods; no hidden simulator quantities enter the objective.
- **Explicit local gauge convention:** keep all 34 stored coordinates, hold `params[0]` (base-twist x) at its starting value, and optimize the remaining 33. Fit diagnostics report full/free counts and the fixed index/value. This selects a representative of the known base/axis/tool gauge orbit; it does not measure true base roll. Phase C confirms the local rank for the tested configurations; other data/parameters can still have additional degeneracy. No axis/tool/home parameterization or public signature was changed.
- **Scaling and solver:** solve in dimensionless coordinates `z_i=params_i/scale_i`. Characteristic scales: base angles/axis tilts/tool angles `0.01 rad`, base translation `1 mm`, tool translation `0.5 mm`, linear/angular joint-distortion coefficients `0.2 mm / 0.01 rad`. Use SciPy trust-region reflective least squares (`trf`, dense SVD subproblems, three-point central numerical Jacobian), linear loss, `ftol=xtol=gtol=1e-10`, default `max_nfev=100`. This avoids explicit normal-matrix inversion. Report callback iterations, solver evaluations, **all** residual calls including finite differences/initial validation, optimality, termination reason, and wall time separately.
- Scales are not physical bounds. Full-coordinate `bounds` and positive `parameter_scales` can be supplied, along with solver evaluation/tolerance/verbosity options. Default bounds are infinite: gauge-dependent axis/tool components cannot automatically be constrained as though they were the simulator's hidden-coordinate parameters. A failed/nonconverged attempt leaves the prior model intact; a converged finite, non-increasing-cost result updates `self.params`. Solver success does not imply physical identifiability, appropriate model family, or held-out accuracy.

### Step 8 Phase C: actual-model rank, gauge, and sensitivity

- **Method [DERIVED/ENGINEERING]:** `jacobian_rank_report` calls the actual Phase B signed, target-weighted residual function. With the default Phase B scales `S`, compute `J_z=dr/dz=J_p S` using central differences at scaled steps `h_i=1e-4*max(1,abs(p_i/S_i))` and `2*h_i`. The report returns physical/scaled Jacobians, full spectra/right directions, and the difference between the two steps. Analyze the full 34 columns and the 33 columns remaining with `params[0]` fixed, without changing the model or solver. SVDs use dimensionless coordinates; condition numbers of mixed-unit raw columns are not physically comparable without scaling.
- **Rank threshold:** `tau=max(1e-7*s_max, 5*||J(h)-J(2h)||_2, eps*max(m,n)*s_max)`. The two-step term is a numerical-error estimate, not a rigorous error bound; the relative tolerance is an engineering decision. Both step sizes and relative cutoffs `1e-8,1e-7,1e-6` give the same ranks in the tested nondegenerate cases. Numerical derivatives can make a true null singular value nonzero: a machine-epsilon-only rank test reports 34 for the nonzero fixture, incorrectly interpreting differentiation noise as information. Residuals near the rotation-log branch cut at pi are rejected rather than differentiated across the discontinuity.
- **Physical gauge [DERIVED]:** for `Q=Rot(x,gamma)`, transform `F_X -> F_X Q`, all imperfect axes `d_k -> Q^-1 d_k`, and the complete wrist-to-EE transform `W -> Q^-1 W`. The exact first axis is unchanged, and `F_CW` (translation along x) commutes with `Q`; therefore tracker-frame predictions do not change. At nominal parameters the tangent has `+1` in base rotation x and `-1` in axis-2 x, axis-3 x, axis-5 x, axis-6 x, and tool rotation x, with other entries zero. At nonzero parameters its coordinates include exponential-chart coupling; the method computes that orbit's tangent independently of the residual SVD and checks its alignment with the nullspace.
- **Practical sensitivity:** report all observable modes below `0.01*s_max` (or the three weakest if fewer qualify), their dominant scaled components, and physical-coordinate directions. This relative label is not a measurement-noise identifiability threshold. `1/s_i` is the gain for a hypothetical unit-variance isotropic weighted residual, not an actual standard error: target weights are not covariance whitening, and real pose noise is anisotropic/correlated with fixed tracker bias. Full numerical rank does not establish precise, unbiased physical coefficients or global uniqueness.

## Meaningful Limitations
- Coverage bounds and plots use nominal kinematics and placement. They do not establish accuracy or coverage under the simulator's unknown base, axis, tool, and joint errors, nor do they establish a feasible calibration-time budget. The 3375-pose sweep is a geometry check, not a calibration acquisition plan.
- Synthetic recovery, the rank audit and Phase D/E tracker validation concern one model/seed, not official physical accuracy or generalization across robot seeds. Physical parameter reporting is implemented and joint-map bounds are enforced by default. The provided IK retains the documented singularity/limit issues; Step 9 physical FK passes its fixed seed-1 grid and Step 10 physical IK passes the prescribed 40 seed-1 targets; multi-seed/general IK validation and optional marker refinement remain pending. Phase C sensitivity gains use target weights, not a measured covariance model.
- Metric inputs are assumed to be valid rigid Frames. SciPy's matrix conversion can orthogonalize small rotation-matrix drift; this metric is not a substitute for independently checking the SO(3) validity of a future fitted model. The scalar angle does not supply a signed rotation axis at pi, which Step 4 does not require.
- Registration numerical rank checks detect unobservable geometry, but do not guarantee good conditioning under measurement noise; spread-out markers remain important. The unweighted chordal frame mean assumes estimates of the same transform. Widely dispersed rotations can have nonunique means (SVD returns one minimizer); it does not account for unequal uncertainty or remove fixed systematic bias. Translation is averaged in the common target frame, independently of orientation, rather than with an SE(3) intrinsic mean.
- The 11-reading rule reduces independent random noise, not fixed tracker distortion or joint-model mismatch. For this centered tetrahedron, the tracker field's specified Lipschitz bound alone permits a **linearized** orientation-bias bound `omega_trk*sqrt(sum||a_i||^2/lambda_min(G)) = 9.19e-4 rad`; arbitrary independent bounded marker errors give a looser `1.89e-3 rad` bound. Translation bias can be as large as `0.1 mm`. Thus the supplied bounds plus this plan do not guarantee the complete `0.2 mm / 0.001 rad` requirement, particularly after parameter fitting and inverse execution. One-dimensional sweep profiles do not identify an arbitrary nonseparable 3-D tracker field; systematic-error sensitivity and held-out workspace tests remain necessary.
